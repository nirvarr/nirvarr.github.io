Research

Background bias in CNN ripeness classification

2025 to 2026

Master's thesis, Universiti Malaya. Supervisor: Assoc. Prof. Dr. Norjihan Binti Abdul Ghani

Unripe golden berries are usually photographed among foliage; ripe ones as harvested produce. A classifier trained on such images can score well by recognising the setting. I tested whether it does.

  • Built a 1,124-image ripe/unripe corpus from web sources and original photography, deduplicated by perceptual hashing, hand-labelled and split 70:15:15.
  • Fine-tuned ResNet50 (98.8%) and MobileNetV2 (97.7%), trained a custom CNN (97.7%), and compared all three against a whole-image colour-histogram baseline (95.9%).
  • Found the planned border-ring diagnostic uninterpretable (the border was fruit-free in only 2 of 109 ripe test images) and replaced it with a background-randomisation control: segmented fruit composited onto backgrounds chosen without reference to the label.
  • Under the control the baseline fell to 82.5% while the pretrained networks held 94.2%; neither network separated from the baseline on unmodified images, and both did on composites (p < 0.01).
